How the record is kept

Nipissing Public Record is an independent archive. It is not run by, paid for, or affiliated with the Township of Nipissing.

About this archive

Why it exists

The township’s website is the official source for council records, but documents come down as they age, and many were removed when the site was rebuilt in June 2026. This archive copies agendas, minutes, by-laws and resolutions as they are published, so they stay available to anyone who wants to read them.

What’s covered

Township of Nipissing Council and its committees, and the shared and district boards the township sits on:

  • Township of Nipissing Council, Committee of Adjustment, Recreation Committee, Museum Board and Cemetery Committee
  • Powassan & District Union Public Library Board
  • Powassan Nipissing Callander OPP Detachment Board
  • District of Nipissing Social Services Administration Board, its committees, and the Nipissing District Housing Corporation Board
  • North Bay Parry Sound District Health Unit Board of Health and its committees

Meetings are covered from 2022 on. Agendas are shown from 2026 on; for earlier meetings, see the minutes and agenda packages.

Where the documents come from

Everything here is already public. Documents are collected from the township’s website, each board’s own site, and the township’s YouTube channel for meeting recordings. The archive checks those sources every morning and adds what’s new.

What “Copy kept” means

When a document is found, a copy is saved in this archive. Links marked Copy kept open that copy, so they keep working if the township removes the original. Links marked “Township site” still point at the original because no copy has been saved yet.

Some documents were removed before they could be copied. Until the end of 2026, older copies are being recovered from the Internet Archive where they exist. Otherwise the meeting is listed with whatever survives, often just the recording.

Summaries and laws in force

Meeting summaries are written by AI from the minutes. They are a quick way in, not the record: the minutes are.

The “Laws in force” pages take a by-law and every by-law that amended it and assemble the text as it reads today, with each change marked. They are prepared with AI from the original documents and checked, but they are unofficial. For anything with legal weight, rely on the original by-laws linked in each page’s history, or ask the township.

The township publishes by-laws in the agenda package presented to council, often marked “draft”. Those copies are treated as the by-law as passed; any change made at the meeting is recorded in the minutes.

Something missing or wrong?

If a meeting, by-law or document is missing, or a summary gets something wrong, report it on GitHub with a link or a description. If you have a copy of a document the township no longer publishes, that’s especially welcome.

The code that collects and publishes the archive is open: github.com/chriswjohnston/Nipissing.
